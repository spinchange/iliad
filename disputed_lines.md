# Manuscript Variants and Disputed Lines in the Iliad

Reference notes compiled alongside the Greek text (`iliad_greek_full.txt` / `books/`) for translation work. The base text in this project is the Perseus digitization of the Monro-Allen edition, which does **not** include an apparatus criticus (the scholarly record of manuscript variants). This document summarizes where to find that information and lists the most significant disputed passages.

**This project now has a local apparatus criticus**: `apparatus/book_01.txt`–`book_24.txt` contain an OCR-extracted apparatus from the 1920 Monro-Allen OCT (see `apparatus/README.md` for the abbreviation legend and known limitations). Each disputed-line entry below notes whether it has a matching entry in these local files. The apparatus is a mechanical extraction — Greek lemma text is sometimes garbled and a handful of entries are filed under an adjacent line number rather than their own exact range (see caveat under §2) — so treat it as a rough index, not a substitute for the sources below when precision matters.

**Note on three lines genuinely absent from the primary Greek text** (discovered 2026-07, while re-checking the source XML for an unrelated reason): `books/book_09.txt` is missing lines **9.458–461** (the Phoenix/patricide passage — see row 7 below), `books/book_11.txt` is missing line **11.543**, and `books/book_14.txt` is missing line **14.269**. These are not extraction errors — the underlying Perseus TEI-XML source this project draws from omits these specific lines from its running text entirely (verified by checking the raw source XML directly; the lines are absent from the `<div>` for each book, not merely mis-parsed). All three are plausibly lines excluded by the source edition's editors as spurious/interpolated, consistent with the athetesis pattern already documented for 9.458–461. If you need the Greek of these specific lines, they are not present anywhere in this project's files and would need to be sourced separately (e.g. Leaf's edition, which prints athetized lines bracketed rather than omitting them). Separately: an unrelated bug in this project's original Greek-text extraction script (it missed `<l>` elements nested inside `<q>` quoted-speech wrappers, silently dropping most direct-speech lines project-wide) was found and fixed on the same date — `iliad_greek_full.txt` and all `books/book_NN.txt` files were regenerated and are now complete aside from the three genuine absences just described.

## 1. Where to find actual manuscript variants

The modern standard critical editions with a full apparatus — M.L. West's Teubner (1998–2000) and Hartmut Erbse's edition of the scholia, *Scholia Graeca in Homerum Iliadem* (Berlin, 1969–1988) — are both still under copyright and not freely available online.

Free alternatives:

- **Monro & Allen, *Homeri Opera*, 3rd ed. (OCT, 1920)** — the direct ancestor of the Perseus base text used in this project, but the original OCT printing includes Allen's concise critical apparatus at the foot of each page. Public domain, fully downloadable.
  [archive.org/details/homerioperarecog04homeuoft](https://archive.org/details/homerioperarecog04homeuoft)
- **Walter Leaf, *The Iliad*, ed. with apparatus criticus, prolegomena, notes, and appendices** (Macmillan, 1900–1902, 2 vols.) — a genuine apparatus criticus with commentary, public domain.
  [Vol. 1](https://archive.org/details/iliadvolume00leafgoog) · [Vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/iliadhom02home) · [plain text](https://archive.org/stream/iliadvolume00leafgoog/iliadvolume00leafgoog_djvu.txt)
- **Jacob La Roche, *Homeri Ilias ad fidem librorum optimorum*** (Teubner, 1873) — 19th-c. critical edition, public domain.
  [archive.org/details/homeriiliasadfid00home](https://archive.org/details/homeriiliasadfid00home)
- **Homer Multitext Project** ([homermultitext.org](https://www.homermultitext.org/)) — the best free resource for actual manuscript-level variants. Rather than one collated apparatus, it publishes independent diplomatic transcriptions of witnesses, spatially linked to page images, in TEI-XML (CC BY-NC-SA).
  - **Venetus A** (Marciana Gr. 454 = 822, 10th c.) — the single most important Iliad manuscript: oldest complete text plus extensive scholia derived from Zenodotus's, Aristophanes of Byzantium's, and Aristarchus's ancient commentaries. [Introduction](https://www.homermultitext.org/manuscripts/venetusA/VenA-Introduction-2014/) · [manuscript page](https://www.homermultitext.org/manuscripts/venetusA/)
  - Also digitized: **Venetus B** (11th c.) and **Genavensis 44** ("Geneva Iliad," 13th c.), each with their own scholia.
  - Downloadable plain-text/Markdown/PDF of the Venetus A text and scholia: [homermultitext.org/texts.html](https://www.homermultitext.org/texts.html)
  - Raw data: [github.com/homermultitext/hmt-reader](https://github.com/homermultitext/hmt-reader) (CEX format)
  - Caveat: Greek only, no English apparatus layer.
- **Perseus/Scaife Viewer** also hosts the *Scholia in Iliadem* (Greek only): [scaife.perseus.org/library/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg5026.tlg001/](https://scaife.perseus.org/library/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg5026.tlg001/)

**Practical note**: the free Monro-Allen 1920 OCT scan is the cheapest upgrade from the current bare text, since it's the same text family with apparatus attached.

## 3. The scholia — what they are and how to access them

The **scholia** are marginal/interlinear annotations copied into medieval manuscripts (occasionally papyri), ultimately deriving from the critical and exegetical work of the Alexandrian scholars — Zenodotus (3rd c. BCE), Aristophanes of Byzantium, and Aristarchus (2nd c. BCE) — and later grammarians. They fall into conventional families:

- **Scholia Vetera** ("older scholia") — the textual-critical layer: variant readings, athetesis, accentuation, reconstruction of Aristarchus's edition. Best preserved in **Venetus A**.
- **D-scholia** — mostly glosses on rare words and mythological background; more lexicon/mythography than textual criticism.
- **bT (exegetical) scholia** — literary/interpretive commentary on plot, character, and poetic technique, best preserved in the Townley manuscript and the "b" family.

Venetus A's scholia are the richest source for recovering Aristarchus's specific readings and judgments, since his own commentaries do not survive independently — only through scholiastic citation. ([Antigone Journal: "Scholiastic Triumphs"](https://antigonejournal.com/2022/04/homer-iliad-scholia/))

**Accessibility**: the modern critical edition (Erbse, 7 vols., 1969–1988) is not freely available, and there is **no complete English translation** of the scholia — existing translations cover only individual scholia quoted piecemeal in secondary scholarship. For a translator without fluency in reading scholia directly in Greek, the practical path is: (a) the Homer Multitext's free Venetus A transcriptions (primary source, Greek only), and (b) secondary literature (BMCR reviews, CHS publications, journal articles) that quote and explain specific cruxes in English.

## Practical takeaways

1. **Cheapest immediate upgrade** from the current bare text: the free Monro-Allen 1920 OCT scan on archive.org, which has more apparatus information and is directly comparable (same base text family).
2. **For real manuscript-level variants**: Homer Multitext's Venetus A transcriptions are the only free, primary, systematically organized resource — but require reading scholia in Greek.
3. **For a curated, English-language guide to the "big" disputes**: secondary scholarship (BMCR reviews, CHS chapters, journal articles cited above) is more efficient than extracting judgments directly from the apparatus.
4. **West's Teubner text is itself a disputed artifact** — his choices about which "plus verses" and repeated passages to keep or cut are controversial among Homerists. If later consulted, it shouldn't be treated as simply "more authoritative" without also weighing the counter-arguments (Nagy, Nardelli reviews).
