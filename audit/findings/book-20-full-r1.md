## Summary

Book 20 — the gods sent down to war, the Aeneas duel, and the
first panel of the aristeia — was read entire against the Greek.
Seven firm findings, all fixed; this book was the audit's richest
harvest of straggler formulas because its killing-run re-uses
Books 5, 11, 12, and 16 death-lines wholesale, and those books
were translated months apart. Fixes reached into Books 5 (three
lines), 11, 12, and 21.

Verified repetitions: 42-43 carry the "because Achilles / had
appeared, who had long stayed out of the grievous fighting"
clause verbatim with 18.247-248 and 19.45-46; 176 "And when they
had come close, advancing on each other" is uniform poem-wide
(20.176, 21.148, 22.248, 23.816); 200-202 (Aeneas's opening) =
431-433 (returned by Hector) kept identical, as the commentary
promises; 449-454 = 11.362-367 verbatim, all six lines of the
"Dog! Again you have run out from under death" cry; 78 keeps
5.289's "glut ... Ares, the fighter under the ox-hide shield";
197-198 = 17.31-32; and the μέγʼ-less ὀχθήσας frame at 343
correctly reads "troubled" where 11.403-family lines read "deeply
troubled."

The content is exact throughout: Themis's total muster with only
Ocean absent; "they matter to me, even as they perish" and the
fear of Achilles storming the wall "ahead of what is fated"; the
seismic tableau with Hades leaping from his throne lest his
"terrible, mouldering houses, which the very gods abhor" stand
naked; the pairings and the anticlimactic sitting-down; Apollo in
Lycaon's voice; Aeneas's lesser-goddess genealogy argument
returned by Apollo; Hera's intervention debate and Poseidon's
refusal; the wall of Heracles; the lion simile entire; Achilles'
taunt (cattle of Ida, Lyrnessus); the Dardanid genealogy complete
— Erichthonius's three thousand mares, the Boreas foals running
on wheat-ears and breakers, Ganymede, the twin branches — with
the twisty-tongue and quarreling-women codas; the five-fold
shield (two bronze, two tin, one gold) stopping the spear; the
stone, the sword, and Poseidon's rescue with the destiny of the
race of Dardanus ("his children's children"); the mist, the
vaulted flight over the ranks, and Achilles' baffled soliloquy;
the exhortations; Apollo's warning to Hector; and the twelve-man
killing run intact — Iphition under the wheel-rims, Demoleon's
spattered brain, Hippodamas's bull-bellow, Polydorus holding his
bowels, the sting of Hector's near-miss with Athena's breath and
Apollo's mist, Dryops, Demuchus, the sons of Bias, Tros of the
refused supplication with the litotes kept ("not a sweet-tempered
man, not mild of mind"), Mulius ear to ear, Echeclus, Deucalion's
flying head with the marrow, Rhigmus and his aide — closed by the
forest-fire and threshing-floor similes and the gore-spattered
hands, mid-carnage, uncadenced, exactly as the Greek leaves it.
